\begin{abstract}
We present the setup and first results of a Fisher-matrix forecast based
on the two-point clustering of an HOD galaxy sample split by cosmic-web
environment with the ASTRA classification.  Derivatives with respect to
the cosmological parameters come from central differences over the
AbacusSummit linear derivative grid; the covariance comes from 64
subboxes of the fiducial simulation.  With the $\omega_b$ pair complete,
we compare the constraining power of the individual auto- and
cross-correlations.  The cross-correlation of the full sample with the
overdense environment quantiles carries slightly more information than
the full-sample auto-correlation itself.  All absolute uncertainties are
still optimistic because the available HOD catalogs are not matched
across cosmologies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}

For a single parameter $\theta$, the Fisher information of a data vector
$\boldsymbol{\xi}$ with covariance $\mathsf{C}$ is
\begin{equation}
F \;=\;
\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial \theta}^{\!\top}
\mathsf{C}^{-1}
\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\xi}}{\partial \theta},
\qquad
\sigma(\theta) \;=\; F^{-1/2}
\label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
\cite{tegmark1997}.  Our data vectors are built from the redshift-space
2PCF multipoles ($\ell = 0, 2$; 15 bins over $0 < s < 150\Mpch$) of the
full LRG-like sample, of the four ASTRA environment quantiles (Q1 = most
underdense, Q4 = most overdense), and of their cross-correlations with
the full sample.  The measurement pipeline (Landy--Szalay estimator over
a $4\times4\times4$ grid of $500\Mpch$ subboxes) is described in the
companion zero-crossing note.

The derivative for each parameter comes from the simulation pair that
brackets the fiducial cosmology.  We difference the \emph{same} subbox
$i$ in the two cosmologies before averaging,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\xi}_i}{\partial \theta}
\;=\;
\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}_i(\theta + \Delta\theta)
    - \boldsymbol{\xi}_i(\theta - \Delta\theta)}{2\,\Delta\theta}.
\label{eq:centraldiff}
\end{equation}
Because all derivative-grid simulations share the initial-condition
phases of the fiducial box, and our pipelines also share all random
seeds, most of the cosmic variance cancels in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:centraldiff}); the paired estimate is about twice as
precise as differencing independent volumes.  The mean over the 64
subboxes gives the derivative and its scatter gives the derivative
uncertainty.

The covariance $\mathsf{C}$ is estimated from the 64 fiducial subboxes.
An inverse covariance built from $n$ samples and $p$ bins is biased, so
we apply the standard correction $(n - p - 2)/(n - 1)$
\cite{hartlap2007}.  Noise in the derivative adds spurious Fisher
information with expectation
$\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{C}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{d})$, where
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{d}$ is the covariance of the derivative estimate;
we subtract this term and quote it as the ``noise share'' of each data
vector.

Figure~\ref{fig:covariances} shows the covariance matrices that enter
Eq.~(\ref{eq:fisher}), in their normalised form
$R_{ij} = \mathsf{C}_{ij} / \sqrt{\mathsf{C}_{ii}\mathsf{C}_{jj}}$
(the raw covariance spans too many orders of magnitude to display).
The single-statistic matrices show the expected structure: neighbouring
separation bins are strongly correlated, and the correlation length
grows toward large $s$, where the same long-wavelength modes dominate
every bin.  The concatenated vector makes the cross-blocks visible.
The full auto and the full $\times$ data Q4 blocks are almost perfectly
correlated with each other --- this is the redundancy discussed in
Section 4.  In contrast, the full $\times$ random Q1 block is strongly
\emph{anti}-correlated with the other two: when a subbox scatters high
in clustering amplitude, its underdense-environment cross scatters low.
This anti-correlation is the only genuinely complementary information
in the concatenation.

\paragraph{From $\sigma$ to the plotted curves.}
The curves we show later are not measured posteriors; they are the
likelihood implied by the Fisher formalism.  In one dimension the
Fisher approach expands the log-likelihood to second order around its
maximum, which is placed at the fiducial value $\theta_{\rm fid}$.  The
quadratic term is the Fisher information $F$, and every higher-order
term is dropped, so the likelihood is \emph{by construction} a Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;\propto\;
\exp\!\left[ -\tfrac{1}{2} F \,(\theta - \theta_{\rm fid})^{2} \right]
\;=\;
\exp\!\left[ -\frac{(\theta - \theta_{\rm fid})^{2}}{2\,\sigma^{2}}
\right],
\qquad \sigma = F^{-1/2}.
\label{eq:gauss}
\end{equation}
This is exact when the data are Gaussian-distributed and the model
$\boldsymbol{\xi}(\theta)$ is linear in $\theta$ over the range of
interest; otherwise it is the leading approximation.  We plot
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gauss}) normalised to unit peak, so only the width carries
information.  When the derivative is taken with respect to a logarithm,
such as $\ln\omega_b$, the width in the physical parameter follows from
the same expansion: $\sigma(\omega_b) = \omega_{b,{\rm fid}}\,
\sigma(\ln\omega_b)$.

\section{Simulations}

Table~\ref{tab:cosmologies} lists the nine simulations of the design:
the fiducial cosmology and the four $\pm$ pairs of the AbacusSummit
linear derivative grid \cite{maksimova2021}, all at phase
\texttt{ph000}, $z = 0.5$.  Each is populated with an LRG-like HOD
catalog ($\sim 4\times10^{6}$ galaxies) from the EMC emulator set.  In
that set, every cosmology carries its own random draws from the HOD
prior, so no identical HOD exists across cosmologies.  The catalogs in
Table~\ref{tab:cosmologies} were selected to minimise the HOD-parameter
mismatch inside each $\pm$ pair around a common mid-prior anchor point
(pair distances 0.14--0.16 of the prior range, against 0.40 for random
draws).  Galaxy number density is matched by construction.

The residual HOD mismatch matters.  The measured
\texttt{c100}$-$\texttt{c101} difference is a scale-dependent $-6\%$ to
$+9\%$ tilt of $\xi_0$, while the physical $\omega_b$ signal at fixed
$\sigma_8$ and $\theta_\ast$ is only $\sim 1\%$.  The derivative is
therefore still dominated by the different galaxy--halo connections of
the two catalogs, and it is not a flat offset, so a simple amplitude
nuisance cannot absorb it completely.  All absolute uncertainties in
this note are too small for this reason; we read them as a
\emph{relative} comparison between data vectors.  A planned correction
is to measure the HOD response $\partial\xi/\partial\theta_{\rm HOD}$
from additional existing fiducial catalogs and subtract it.

\section{Derivatives}

Figure~\ref{fig:derivatives} shows the central-difference derivative of
every statistic with respect to $\ln\omega_b$, from
Eq.~(\ref{eq:centraldiff}) applied to the 64 paired subboxes.  The
derivative of the full-sample auto-correlation is detected with a
signal-to-noise ratio of up to $\sim 19$ per bin at small separations;
at $s \gtrsim 80\Mpch$ several bins are consistent with zero.  The
quantile statistics inherit the same tilt with larger noise.  These are
the inputs to Eq.~(\ref{eq:fisher}).

Three lessons emerge.  First, the cross-correlations of the full sample
with the overdense quantiles (Q3, Q4) carry slightly more information
than the full-sample auto-correlation itself: the cross keeps the low
shot noise of the full sample while the environment selection sharpens
the response.  Second, the quadrupole adds almost nothing for
$\omega_b$: doubling the number of bins costs more through the
$(n-p-2)/(n-1)$ factor than the new bins contribute.  Third,
concatenating vectors that share the full-sample leg (full auto
$\oplus$ full $\times$ data Q4 $\oplus$ full $\times$ random Q1) ties
with the full auto alone, even after compressing each piece from 15 to
8 bins to soften the covariance penalty: the pieces are too correlated.
Real gains must come from independent tracers in the data vector or
from more covariance realisations.

\section{Next steps}

The remaining pairs of Table~\ref{tab:cosmologies} complete the
4-parameter design; the same scripts process them automatically.  The
$\sigma_8$ pair is the most important test: its physical signal
($\sim 8\%$ in amplitude across the pair) should dominate both the
noise and the residual HOD contamination, unlike $\omega_b$.  The two
structural improvements identified here are an HOD-response correction
built from additional fiducial catalogs, and a larger set of covariance
realisations so that longer concatenated data vectors become usable.
