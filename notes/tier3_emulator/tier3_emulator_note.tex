\begin{abstract}
The Fisher work (see the data-vector-search note) shows ASTRA quantile clustering
can tighten $\omega_b,\omega_c,n_s$ by several-fold over the 2PCF, but that the
gain lives almost entirely at small scales ($s\lesssim40\Mpch$).  Turning this into
\emph{measured} constraints needs a forward model $\xi(\theta)$ over the whole
prior --- an emulator trained on a space-filling cosmology grid --- because a
Fisher forecast only needs local derivatives, and small scales cannot be modelled
analytically.  This note records the Tier-3 design and the pilot.
\end{abstract}

\section{What changes versus Fisher}
Fisher needs derivatives at one fiducial, so the nine-cosmology derivative star
suffices.  Inference needs $\xi(\theta)$ everywhere in the prior, i.e.\ a
space-filling cosmology grid plus an emulator.  The catalogs exist: the source has
\textbf{85 cosmologies} (\code{c000}--\code{c181}; we have used 9) and 500 HODs
each (we have used 50).  Tier-3 is therefore a compute problem, not a data one.

\section{Design, driven by the findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cosmologies: the space-filling block \code{c130}--\code{c181}}
  ($\sim$52, the AbacusSummit emulator Latin hypercube).  It varies the
  \emph{broader} parameter set (\,$w_0,w_a,N_{\rm eff}$, running\,), so the
  emulator inputs must include those ($\sim$8-D cosmology), not just the four
  $\Lambda$CDM parameters.  The nine derivative cosmologies are held out as
  validation anchors.
\item \textbf{HODs: $\sim$100--150 per cosmology} from the unused 500.  The HOD
  response is effectively $\sim$4-D (\code{SIGMA}, \code{LOGM\_CUT} dominate) and
  50 draws already captured $\sim$75\%, so $\sim$120 is ample.  Total
  $\sim$52$\times$120 $\approx$ 6000 training runs.
\item \textbf{Focused, small-scale data vector.}  The information is at
  $s\lesssim40\Mpch$ and in the extreme-environment crosses (void/knot/filament,
  data and random).  Emulate a reduced vector of those pieces, \emph{finely
  binned at small scales} ($\sim$2--4$\Mpch$, vs the current 10$\Mpch$), not all
  17 stems over 0--150.
\item \textbf{Phases / covariance --- a real constraint.}  Single phase
  (\code{ph000}) is adequate for the emulator \emph{mean} (the gain is at small
  scales, where cosmic variance is small).  The \emph{covariance}, however, is the
  hard part.  We verified what is on disk: the \code{ntbfin} HOD catalogs have
  \emph{only} \code{ph000} for every cosmology --- no extra phases and no
  small-box galaxy catalogs.  The underlying AbacusSummit \emph{halo} catalogs
  \emph{are} on CFS (\code{/global/cfs/cdirs/desi/cosmosim/Abacus}: 25 base phases
  \code{ph000}--\code{ph024}, and the 1883-box \code{small/} suite, the standard
  covariance set), but turning them into galaxy catalogs would mean
  \textbf{populating new HODs}, which is explicitly \emph{out of scope} (no new
  HOD generation).  Consequences:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{derivative} side of Tier-3 is fully reachable: the emulator
    grid \code{c130}--\code{c181} \emph{is} HOD-populated at \code{ph000} (that is
    what the pilot runs on).
  \item The \textbf{covariance} stays the \emph{subbox} estimate (the pooled
    $500\Mpch$ subboxes of the \code{ph000} boxes, $\sim$576 samples, with its
    open-boundary / non-independence / $\sim$10--20\% absolute caveats), unless an
    \emph{externally pre-populated} galaxy mock suite (e.g.\ EZmocks) is adopted.
    The ideal 1883 independent small-box covariance is therefore \emph{not}
    available under the no-new-HOD constraint.  This is the main residual weakness
    of Tier-3 as a measured-constraints path: the emulator fixes the derivatives,
    but the covariance remains approximate.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Iterations.}  Run the full campaign at $\sim$10 ASTRA iterations to
  lower the per-vector noise floor (the random legs benefit).  The \emph{pilot}
  uses 3 iterations (cheap, $\sim$25 min, fits the 1-h queue limit) since the
  small scales are already signal-rich.
\item \textbf{Architecture.}  $\sim$6000 points over $\sim$20-D input
  $\Rightarrow$ an MLP (SUNBIRD-style), not a GP (which dies past a few-thousand
  points).  PCA or direct multi-output, covariance-weighted MSE loss (matches the
  inference metric), an ensemble for the emulator uncertainty, and
  \emph{leave-one-cosmology-out} validation (cosmology is the sparse axis).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The pilot (this note's deliverable)}
A small batch to decide go/no-go \emph{before} the full campaign:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Runs:} 10 cosmologies spread over \code{c130}--\code{c181}
  $\times$ 50 maximin HODs $=$ 500 full-box runs at 3 iterations, into the
  isolated root \code{data/fullbox\_tier3/} (so the Fisher dataset is untouched).
  \code{scripts/select\_tier3\_pilot.py} + \code{queue/launch\_tier3\_pilot.sh}.
\item \textbf{The one question it must answer:} can an emulator reach
  \emph{sub-noise accuracy at $s<40\Mpch$} for the environment crosses, validated
  leave-one-cosmology-out?  The entire Tier-3 payoff rides on this.
\item \textbf{If yes:} run the full \code{c130}--\code{c181}$\times$120 campaign at
  10 iterations, build the MLP, then MCMC on held-out mocks (does it recover truth;
  does the measured $\sigma$ match the Fisher forecast --- it will be looser, esp.\
  $\sigma_8$), then apply to data.
\item \textbf{If no:} fall back to the conservative-scale ($s>40\Mpch$) result,
  $\sim$1.5$\times$ on $\omega_b,\omega_c,n_s$, with analytic modelling and no
  emulator.
\end{itemize}

\section{Compute}
Pilot: 500 runs $\times$ 3 iters ($\sim$25 min) $\approx$ 200 node-hours, backfills
in $\sim$1--2 days.  Full campaign: 6000 runs $\times$ 10 iters ($\sim$1.5 h)
$\approx$ 9000 node-hours --- a few weeks of queue, hence the pilot first.
